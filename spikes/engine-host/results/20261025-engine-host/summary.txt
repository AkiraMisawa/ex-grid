== Reading ==

- Where Blazor WebAssembly's 3.3 s goes. About three quarters of it is the baseline's configuration,
  not the engine: Bench.Host under `dotnet run` serves the build output, whose .NET runtime is set up
  for debugging (debugLevel -1), and the interpreter runs C# several times slower there. Published, the
  same page edits A1 in 1,072 ms, not 4,069 ms (this container), and L1 in 172 ms, not 430. The marks
  in blazor-edit-trace.txt show it step by step: Workbook.Do takes 3,507 ms in the build output and
  784 ms published; ExGrid's PrepareRender 152 and 30; ToDocument 124 and 53.
- Of the published 1.07 s, the engine is most: Workbook.Do 784 ms (the probe's 951 ms on the
  interpreter in the Worker is the same order, on a cold page). Around it ExSheet's Blazor side costs
  about 200 ms an edit whatever the edit reaches: SheetEdges.Update 26 ms over the 20,000 Values
  changed, ExGrid's parameters and render about 100 ms (PrepareRender 30 ms over the Sheet's 16,384
  columns), ToDocument 53 ms for the two-way binding, the page's render of it 17 ms. That is why L1,
  which recalculates nothing, still takes 172 ms on Blazor WebAssembly against 17 ms in the Worker.
- The Worker is the Blazor page's equal on the interpreter and twice as fast compiled ahead of time.
  Edit A1: 967 ms interpreted, 513 ms AOT, against 1,072 ms published Blazor. L1: 17 ms and 10 ms.
  Scrolling the Sheet holds 17.6 ms frames (p95) against 33 ms on published Blazor: the Script Core's
  paint against ExSheet's Razor UI, since nothing recalculates while the driver scrolls. That the page
  keeps its frame while the Worker recalculates is the Worker's purpose and was not measured here. The
  crossing is small: the edit is answered with the Window the grid holds (the rows it needs and 60
  either side, 26 columns of display text), in one message each way.
- AOT against the interpreter: the recalculation 1.7 times faster (probe: 554 against 951 ms; the
  page: 513 against 967), the Sheet's open 2.2 times (607 against 1,318 ms) and the pivot about five
  times (a Window after the columns swapped 24 against 116 ms, the first report Window 21 against 111). On the JIT the recalculation is 162 ms:
  AOT is 3.4 times the JIT, the interpreter 5.9 times.
- ExPivot. A Change Batch of one trade redraws in 15 ms (AOT), 32 ms (interpreter) and 24 ms
  (published Blazor); swapping the columns 37, 115 and 140 ms. The Worker answers a whole report
  Window as PivotReportJson (no Window Changes against a Baseline), so the crossing is the protocol's,
  not a stand-in.
- Download, Brotli, the whole page: 2.74 MB for the Worker interpreted (1.19 MB runtime, 1.05 MB
  framework assemblies, 0.45 MB the five engine packages, 52 KB the page with ExGrid's Script Core);
  5.66 MB compiled ahead of time (4.52 MB of it dotnet.native.wasm, which holds the compiled code);
  4.01 MB for the published Blazor page, which also carries Blazor, ExGrid's, ExSheet's, ExPivot's and
  ExChart's C# UI. The build output Bench.Host serves is 13.0 MB, uncompressed.
- Time to the first answer, from navigation to the first painted cell with text: the Sheet 5.1 s
  interpreted and 1.9 s AOT, against 5.4 s for published Blazor; the pivot 2.1 s and 1.2 s against
  2.0 s. .NET starts in the Worker in 0.4 to 0.6 s; the rest is the first pass over the data on a cold
  runtime: opening the Sheet's 20,000 Formulas (FromJson and the first recalculation) takes 4.5 s of
  engine time interpreted and 1.3 s AOT.
