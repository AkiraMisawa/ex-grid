One or several whole columns selected from their Headings show the fill handle at the top-right, beside the Heading, and whole rows at the bottom-left, as Excel did (observed: one column and one row by the Windows run of 2026-10-11, h3, several by the readings of 2026-10-12, R5 (2)), and every cell (Ctrl+A) shows none, as Excel showed none in view (R5 (1)). Dragged across (down), one whole column (row) fills every row (column) of the target, text copied, a Formula filled, and a lone number going on by one while one cell's handle still copies it, as Excel did (observed, R5 (3) and (4)); dragged along the axis it spans, nothing changes (R5 (3) and (4)). A fill from several whole columns or rows is refused where a line holds a number beside a blank, as any such pattern is. A fill from whole columns or whole rows, by the handle or, over whole rows, by Ctrl+D, records the source's column (row) format on the target columns (rows) and writes only the cells that hold something, so its cost is those cells, not a million: column C made bold and filled into D:E records bold on D and E, where each of their two million cells was written bold before. Ctrl+R over whole columns is refused as too large, with the notice a paste of more than a million cells gives.
